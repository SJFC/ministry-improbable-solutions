Report No. 39 · The ministry of small injustices, with the ministry of sestina logic

The ministry of small injustices uses the movements of starlings and formal poetic logic to solve the problem of parking fines.

Problem (chosen)
Parking fines
Creature (dealt)
Starling
Form (dealt)
Sestina

Parking fines fail because they are fixed: the same car, the same bay, the same resentment. A starling in a murmuration holds no set position. It watches its six or seven nearest neighbours and moves when they move, an echo curved in wing-beats. No starling has ever complained of being ticketed.

The ministry of sestina logic suggests that parking is limited to six bays per street, rotated at dusk in the order 6-1-5-2-4-3. No vehicle occupies the same bay twice in six days. The roles rotate on the same schedule: driver, warden, appellant, magistrate, witness, starling. By the end of the cycle every resident has issued the fine, paid the fine and dismissed it. Revenue is unchanged. Grievance is evenly distributed.

This is enforced by mimicry. Local starlings already reproduce the wailing of the car alarm. They will be trained to reproduce the footfall and paper rustle of the warden.

Risks. Streets with seven cars. The starling is red-listed; the warden is not.

Final notice

At dusk, the warden.
The bay is fine.
The street belongs to the starling.
