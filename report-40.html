Concerning parking fines, with reference to the starling

Problem (chosen)
Parking fines
Creature (dealt)
Starling
Form (dealt)
Ballad

Finding. A fine is forgotten on payment. A ballad is not. It has no author, it travels by mouth, and someone in it always comes to a bad end. Starlings learn sounds from one another and pass them down the roost. The Ministry observes that the infrastructure for oral tradition is already installed above most car parks.

Recommendation. Abolish the fine. Commission one ballad concerning a Mr Alder, who overstayed by eleven minutes and was not seen again. Teach it to a founding flock of forty. Release at dusk.

Enforcement. None required. The ballad is sung nightly over the scene of the offence. He was only stopping for a minute.

Risks. Ballads drift in transmission. In versions now circulating further north, Mr Alder overstays by a year. In one, the warden marries him. In the oldest roosts it is the warden who is not seen again. The Ministry is unable to recall the birds. He was only stopping for a minute.

Signage

He parked upon the double line
at half past three in May;
the starlings sing above the spot
where he is parked today.
